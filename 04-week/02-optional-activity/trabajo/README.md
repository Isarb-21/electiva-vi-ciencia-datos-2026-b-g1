## Problem & data
An agroindustrial crop production company struggles to schedule irrigation efficiently, leading to water waste or reduced yields when crops experience hydric stress. The goal is to use historical and real-time data to identify the soil and climate conditions associated with the best crop performance. The dataset includes at least eight sources: soil moisture sensors, temperature sensors, water consumption meters, rain gauges, crop growth stage records, harvest yield logs, irrigation logbooks, and satellite imagery. Descriptive analytics is the primary approach, since the first step is understanding what conditions have historically produced the best results. Diagnostic analytics complements this by explaining why certain irrigation cycles were less efficient than others. The data variety — structured sensor readings, semi-structured logbooks, and unstructured images — and the critical need for reliable sensor data make this a potential Big Data case. Poor sensor calibration is a concrete data quality risk, as a miscalibrated humidity sensor can trigger incorrect irrigation decisions that directly affect crop yield.
